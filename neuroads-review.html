What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

It launched on 5 June 2026 and, as of 19 August 2026, had six reviews: all five stars, all posted between 7 and 15 August, by merchants who had had it installed for five, eleven and thirteen days. Nothing about that is suspicious on its face; new apps have new reviews. But six enthusiastic first impressions from people two weeks in tell you the setup is smooth and tell you nothing about what happens when the bot gets a refund question wrong in month four.

By 9 October 2026 there were twelve, still all five stars. On the same page, Shopify Inbox is free with 5,488 reviews. In August the listing contradicted itself on the trial: the summary promised a 30-day trial while the pricing table showed seven days. By 9 October 2026 the summary says only 'Free trial available' and all three paid plans show a 7-day free trial, so plan on seven days and check it in the charge screen.

And look at what it asks for. This app requests names, email addresses, phone numbers and physical addresses of your customers, plus geolocation and IP, sixty days of order history, your discount codes and your store pages. That is a defensible list for a bot that tracks orders and offers codes, and it is also a lot of customer data to route through an app launched in June 2026 on the strength of a dozen reviews.

The privacy policy (last updated 10 July 2026) answers part of it: hosting and storage on Amazon Web Services in the US region, order data fetched live rather than stored, and chat transcripts deleted 90 days after a conversation ends. Check that against your own obligations before you switch it on for a store with EU shoppers.

When the paid plan is worth it

The conversation counts are the headline, but they are not where the tiers actually differ. Starter buys you the two things a free chat widget cannot do: hand a live conversation to a human, and hand a shopper a discount code. If you have anyone answering customer email at all, that is the step worth taking, and 800 conversations for $19 is the cheapest row on the ladder per conversation you are likely to use.

Growth at $49 is where measurement arrives: the revenue and ROAS dashboard, greeting A/B tests, answer insights, Klaviyo. It quadruples the conversation allowance for two and a half times the money, so on volume alone it pays off above roughly 1,200 conversations a month. But the honest reason to be on it is the dashboard: without it you are running a sales widget you cannot attribute anything to. Unlimited at $119 is a different purchase again, and two of its lines have nothing to do with volume.

Removing 'Powered by NeuroAds' from your storefront costs $119 a month, six times Starter: that is a branding fee, not a capacity fee. The other is conversation history: a year on Unlimited, unstated on every tier below it. Upgrading for that line does not settle it, though: the developer's privacy policy (last updated 10 July 2026) says chat transcripts are permanently deleted 90 days after a conversation ends. Get the retention on your tier in writing before you pay $119 a month for history you may not keep.

How to actually use NeuroAds

Stay on Free longer than feels necessary. Two hundred conversations a month is enough to see the two things that decide this app: whether the answers about your own shipping and returns policies are right, and how many of those 200 conversations were shoppers rather than bots. Before anything else, feed it your five most awkward real customer questions: a partial refund, a delayed order, a sizing edge case. Read the answers as a customer would, because it scrapes your policy pages in sixty seconds and inherits every ambiguity in them.

Count your conversations for a full month before picking a tier; the ladder is built on a unit the listing never defines, so your own count is the only honest input. If you upgrade, take Starter for the human takeover rather than the volume, and set up who is watching that queue: a handover nobody answers is worse than no handover. And check the trial length in the charge screen: the page says both seven days and thirty.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Unlimited plan really keep a year of chat history?

The sources conflict. The App Store listing (read 9 October 2026) lists '1-year conversation history' on Unlimited ($119 a month or $1,142 a year). NeuroAds' privacy policy (last updated 10 July 2026, read 9 October 2026) says chat transcripts are automatically and permanently deleted 90 days after the conversation ends. Until the developer clarifies in writing, do not buy Unlimited for the history alone.

What happens to my chat data if I uninstall NeuroAds?

According to the privacy policy (read 9 October 2026), the Shopify shop/redact request sent on uninstall makes NeuroAds delete all of the store's data, and order data is never stored because it is fetched live at query time. The listing shows CSV export only on Unlimited; whether lower tiers can export transcripts before you uninstall is not confirmed.

Where is shopper data stored?

Among the processors the privacy policy (read 9 October 2026) names are Amazon Web Services for hosting and data storage in the US region and Langfuse for monitoring the AI pipeline. Chat sessions expire after 15 minutes of inactivity. For a store with EU shoppers that is a transfer to the US, worth checking against your own privacy notice.
