What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

First, calling now runs on the same meter as texting — 1 credit a minute, 2 a minute when forwarding — so voice minutes eat the allowance you budgeted for messages. Second, the plan ladder is a canyon: $25 to $329 a month with nothing between them, and the credits dropdown raises your allowance without adding a single seat. Size the credits, not the headcount. An MMS is 2 credits, so one picture message to 1,000 contacts is 2,000 credits — four times the entire monthly allowance on Basic.

Seats and numbers bill on top of every plan beyond the first of each: a five-person team on one number is $40 a month over the sticker, and the annual headline of $21 and $277 is a per-month display of a bill charged yearly. Note what the page has stopped saying since our previous check: there is no published per-credit overage rate anymore, so what happens when you run out mid-cycle is not stated where you buy — ask before you commit.

And US business texting carries a regulatory layer of its own: 10DLC registration and carrier surcharges bill separately from anything quoted here, and opt-in and opt-out handling stays your legal responsibility — the platform enables compliance, it does not absolve you.

Eight forwarded calls empty the Basic plan

Salesmessage has been repackaged since our last check into two named plans plus Enterprise, and everything is now metered in credits (2026-08-08, re-verified on their own pricing page). Basic is $25 a month for 500 credits; Pro is $329 for 10,000; Enterprise is quoted. Both plans carry a dropdown that raises the allowance for a fixed add-on: on Basic, 1,000 credits is +$24, 2,500 is +$74, 5,000 is +$154 and 7,500 is +$224 a month.

On Pro the ladder runs from 15,000 at +$150 to 50,000 at +$1,170, in 5,000-credit steps. That dropdown matters more than it looks, because the gap between $25 and $329 is usually described as a canyon — including by us. It is not, once you use it: the real ladder is $25, $49, $99, $179, $249, then $329, which is a reasonably smooth climb. The trap is that the middle of the range is a dropdown rather than a plan, so most people never see it and jump straight to Pro.

If you need 5,000 credits, Basic at $179 does it for 46% less than Pro. The unit price barely moves across all of that: $0.050 a credit on Basic's base allowance, $0.033 on Pro's, $0.030 at Pro's 50,000. A hundredfold increase in volume buys a 40% improvement in rate, which is a shallow curve by any standard — so there is no volume argument for buying ahead of your needs.

Now the meter itself, which is where the money actually goes. The page defines it plainly: an SMS is one credit per 160 characters, an MMS is two credits, a call is one credit a minute and call forwarding is two credits a minute. Three consequences follow. Most real marketing texts — a message, a link and an opt-out line — run past 160 characters, so budget two credits each and read Basic's 500 as roughly 250 messages a month.

One picture message to a thousand contacts is 2,000 credits, four times Basic's entire allowance. And because calling now runs on the same meter as texting, eight half-hour forwarded calls exhaust the whole Basic plan before you send a single text. If voice is part of your workflow, the credit count is not a messaging budget at all. Seats and numbers bill separately from every plan: the first of each is free, then $10 a month per extra seat and $5 per extra number, or $100 and $50 a year.

A five-person team on one number is $40 a month over the sticker. Annual billing is shown as two months free — Basic at $21 a month for 6,000 credits a year, Pro at $277 for 120,000 — and note that those are per-month displays of a bill charged yearly. Two gaps to close before you commit. The page no longer publishes a per-credit overage rate, so what happens when you run out mid-cycle is not stated where you buy — ask, in writing.

And US business texting has a regulatory layer that bills outside all of this: 10DLC registration and carrier surcharges are separate, and opt-in and opt-out handling remains your legal responsibility. The platform supports compliance; it does not provide it. There is a 14-day trial and a 30-day money-back guarantee, refunded less the credits you used.

How to actually use Salesmsg

Work out your credit consumption before you pick a plan, because the meter is the product. Count the characters in a real message of yours — most marketing texts with a link and an opt-out line pass 160 and therefore cost two credits, not one. Multiply by your list. Then add voice separately: calls are one credit a minute and forwarded calls two, on the same allowance as your texting.

Open the credits dropdown before you compare plans. The middle of the price range is hidden there rather than published as a tier, so Basic reaches 7,500 credits at $249 — and if 5,000 is your number, Basic at $179 costs 46% less than Pro. Most people never look and jump to $329. Ask two questions in writing during the 14-day trial. What is the per-credit overage rate — the page has stopped publishing it, so what happens when you run out mid-cycle is unknown at the point of purchase.

And confirm what 10DLC registration and carrier surcharges will add, since those bill outside everything quoted on the pricing page. Note that the 30-day money-back guarantee is refunded less the credits you have used, so a heavy first month is not fully reversible.
