What Quo does

What buyers report about Quo

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Quo ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • 4.7 across 3,200+ reviews with over 90,000 businesses on it — the interface is what everyone names, and teams say having calls, texts and voicemail in one shared inbox genuinely organises them.
  • • The September 2025 rebrand from OpenPhone came with $105M in growth funding, so the company is not going anywhere.
What they complain about
  • • The $19 per user headline understates it. A five-person Starter team on annual billing lands around $97-$108 a month rather than the $75 the page implies — 29-44% above sticker.
  • • Sona costs extra past 10 free calls a month, and the AI credit pricing frustrates reviewers at scale.
  • • Support breaks down exactly when it matters: slow responses, unexpected account flags and unresolved tickets during technical problems.
  • • Audio instability and dropped calls on unstable Wi-Fi, which matters if your team works from home.
  • • It suits freelancers and micro-teams. Larger teams report hitting scalability and reliability limits.

The sticker is the smallest number on the bill

There are three tiers now, not two. Starter is $15 per user a month on annual billing and $19 monthly. Business is $23 annual and $33 monthly. Scale, which is new, is $35 annual and $47 monthly. The monthly premium is uneven — 27% on Starter, 43% on Business and 34% on Scale — so the middle plan, the one most teams land on, is the one that punishes monthly billing hardest, and nothing on the page frames it that way.

Then there is everything that is not the sticker. The page says only that applicable taxes and fees are added at checkout, without quantifying them, so a five-person team can price the $75 subscription and nothing else. What is itemised sits lower down: The Campaign Registry takes $19.50 once and $1.50 to $3 a month to keep messaging deliverable to AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon, every extra phone number is $5 a month, and international calling is per-minute on top.

Budget above the sticker and ask for your own tax rate in writing, because there is no way to see it before you buy. The AI agent is the newest meter. Sona answers calls around the clock and is now included on every plan, but it runs on credits: 1,000 automation credits a month, and a call costs 100. That is ten answered calls a month before you are buying more — $25 a month for forty calls, up to $199 for six hundred, with overage from $1.00 down to $0.45 a call.

For a phone system whose whole promise is not missing calls, ten a month is a trial allowance rather than a feature. What that money is buying is where this gets harder to recommend. Call reliability is the recurring complaint rather than an isolated one — G2 carries hundreds of connection and disconnection reports, and calls get shaky the moment you leave Wi-Fi. For a phone system that is not a feature gap, it is the product.

There is also no video conferencing, no 24/7 support on the lower tiers, and the CRM integrations are shallow — the HubSpot app sits at about 3.3 out of 5. So make the reliability question the whole evaluation and let price come second. If calls hold up on your network, in your building, on the connections your team actually uses, Starter on annual billing is a reasonable price for a business line. If they do not, no tier fixes it.

How to actually use Quo

Test call reliability first, and test it badly on purpose. So use the 7-day free trial to make calls from a phone on mobile data, from the building your team actually works in, at the time of day they actually call. Add up the fees that sit outside the sticker before you compare it with anything. The page quantifies none of the taxes — it says only that they are added at checkout — but it does itemise $19.50 once and $1.50 to $3 a month for Campaign Registry messaging registration, $5 a month for every phone number beyond the one per user, and per-minute international calling.

Ask for your own tax rate in writing. Then check the Sona allowance against your actual call volume. Every plan includes 1,000 automation credits a month and an answered call costs 100, so the included allowance is ten calls. If the AI answering is the reason you are buying, price the $25-to-$199 credit tiers in from the start rather than discovering them later. Check the CRM integration on your own CRM rather than on the logo list — the HubSpot app sits at about 3.3 out of 5 — and be clear about what no tier adds: no video conferencing, and no 24/7 support on the lower plans.
