Shopify helpdesk apps: four meters, and only one of them is agents

Researched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-01

Three of these four do not charge per agent, which changes the comparison completely.

Helpdesk pricing is usually per agent, so most buyers arrive at this category with per-agent arithmetic in their head. For Shopify apps that instinct is wrong more often than it is right: the four tools here bill on tickets, on FAQ page views, on agents and on AI conversations respectively. Same category, four different meters.

That matters because the meters reward opposite behaviour. A per-ticket tool gets cheaper when your FAQ page deflects questions; a page-view tool gets more expensive precisely when that page starts working. Knowing which you are on tells you what to optimise after you install it.

How we picked

  • We read prices on the vendor’s own listing or pricing page and date them, and we click through to the monthly column where the table opens on annual.
  • We name the meter first, because in this category the meter is the decision.
  • We say who should skip each app — several of these are Shopify-shaped in ways that make them a poor fit for a business wider than the store.

The picks

1H
HelpCenter
Best for: small Shopify stores that want ticketing, live chat and a searchable FAQ page from one app rather than bolting three tog

The unusual one: agents are unlimited and you are billed for tickets over an allowance. That inverts the normal advice — adding a colleague to the inbox is free, and the way to cut the bill is to answer fewer tickets, which is what its FAQ builder is for.

Skip it if you already run a general helpdesk like Zendesk or Freshdesk for a business wider than the store — this is Shopify-shaped, not company-shaped

StoreFAQ
Best for: small Shopify stores that want SEO-tagged FAQ pages with JSON-LD schema and a light AI chatbot for deflection, without s

Metered on the traffic your FAQ pages receive rather than on how many FAQs you write. Read that sentence twice before adopting it at volume: the meter runs fastest exactly when the pages are doing their job.

Skip it if you have real traffic, because the page-view meter charges you for the FAQ pages doing their job, or you need ticketing, agent workflows and multichannel support reporting

3E
eDesk
Best for: e-commerce and marketplace sellers who want one unified inbox across Amazon, eBay, Shopify and email — with order data a

The conventional meter, and the one to pick if your support spans marketplaces as well as the store. Billed annually by default with a monthly option, and the free plan is a real starting point rather than a demo.

Skip it if you run a non-commerce SaaS or service business (general helpdesks fit better and cheaper), or your ticket volume is low enough that a shared inbox already copes

4T
Tidio
Best for: support teams that want an AI agent and a help desk from the same vendor and can size all three meters — billable conver

Bills on more than one axis, and the plan table opens on the annual column, so the monthly figure is not the one you first see. Workable if you can size all the meters honestly; risky if you cannot, because the over-cap rate is the number that decides the bill.

Skip it if you need the AI agent to keep answering past the first 50 conversations without paying, you cannot get the over-cap rate and the Plus usage rate quoted before signing, you want one per-seat price rather than three independent meters, or you are buying on the strength of the 67 percent resolution figure and need it as a commitment rather than an average

Deflection is a pricing decision, not just a support one

Every question your FAQ answers is a ticket somebody does not have to read. On a per-ticket meter that is money; on a per-agent meter it is time; on a page-view meter it is, awkwardly, a cost. The same improvement therefore pays off differently depending on which app you installed, which is a good argument for choosing the meter before choosing the feature set.

If you are early enough to still be deciding, build the FAQ page first and watch what people still ask afterwards. The residue is your real ticket volume, and it is the number every quote in this category should be sized against.

Frequently asked questions

Is a per-ticket helpdesk cheaper than per-agent?

It depends entirely on the ratio between your team size and your ticket volume. A two-person team drowning in tickets is better off per agent; a larger team with modest volume is usually better off per ticket.

Do I need a separate FAQ app?

Not if your helpdesk builds one, and two of the apps here do. A dedicated FAQ app earns its place when you want the pages indexed and structured for search rather than just answered in a widget.

What should I check before installing an AI chat tier?

The over-cap rate, and whether the AI keeps answering once the included conversations run out. That single line decides whether an AI tier is a fixed cost or a variable one.
