Who should skip it: if you already run a general helpdesk like Zendesk or Freshdesk for a business wider than the store — this is Shopify-shaped, not company-shaped.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

But the meter is tickets, not agents: Basic hands you unlimited support staff and then charges $0.16 for every ticket past fifty. At 300 tickets a month that is $45.99, against $19.99 for the Standard tier that includes them outright — so check your own ticket volume before you treat $5.99 as the price. One dependency worth knowing: translating FAQs into other languages is handed off to a separate app (LangShop), so a multilingual store is two subscriptions, not one.

What HelpCenter does

Shopify helpdesk with live chat and an AI FAQ builder: email, chat and Messenger in one ticket queue, plus SEO-friendly FAQ pages. Free to install; paid from $5.99 to $49.99 a month.

What buyers report about HelpCenter

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in 2026-08-29. We have not run HelpCenter ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • The FAQ builder is what keeps people: reviewers describe running their whole help section on it for four, six, even eight years, with schema markup and deep links to individual questions included.
  • • Live-chat support is named repeatedly and by first name. One merchant who had left a one-star review nine years earlier came back in April 2026 to say chat answered in about thirty seconds and fixed the problem inside ten minutes.
  • • Reviewers on the ticketing side value assigning customer questions to different agents and answering with saved templates, alongside the FAQ and contact form in the same tool.
What they complain about
  • • The 2023 move from free to paid is still the single biggest cluster of one-star reviews. Merchants report their FAQ articles being cut to five on the live storefront — one describes 25 of 30 articles deleted with no way to get them back, another says the content disappeared without notice. The vendor apologised publicly and called the data loss an oversight. If you are on the free tier, treat the article limit as something that touches your live site, not just your admin.
  • • A September 2025 permissions update asked for access to customer and order data. A merchant of four years wrote that this is too much for what they use as an FAQ tool and said they were considering migrating; the vendor replied that the scope does not do what the reviewer feared.
  • • Contact-form submissions do not become tickets — they are emailed instead, which a June 2025 reviewer calls clunky for a helpdesk.
  • • A December 2023 reviewer flags heading structure: categories, sections and articles are three fixed levels, so a store that skips a level ends up with skipped heading levels in the markup. The same reviewer finds the template choice limited.

Where the ratings disagree. The rating is 4.5 across 243 reviews, but the shape matters more than the average: 206 five-stars against nine one-stars, and almost all of those one-stars are dated 2023 and about one event — the pricing change that limited free FAQs. The five-stars are spread across a decade and are mostly about support and the FAQ builder. Read the one-stars as a history of how the vendor handled a plan migration, not as a verdict on the product today.

When the paid plan is worth it

The plan price is not the meter here -- the per-ticket overage is, and it runs the opposite way to what you might expect. Free includes 10 tickets a month and then charges $0.25 each; Basic at $5.99 includes 50 and charges $0.16; Standard at $19.99 includes 300 and charges $0.15; Advanced at $49.99 includes 1,000 and charges $0.10. So the more you pay upfront, the less each extra ticket costs, which means the plan to pick follows from your actual ticket volume rather than from the feature list.

Run the arithmetic: at 200 tickets a month, Basic costs $5.99 plus 150 at $0.16 (about $30), while Standard is a flat $19.99 -- Standard wins well before you reach its 300-ticket ceiling. The feature steps matter less: the real one is Free to Basic, which unlocks unlimited FAQs backed by OpenAI, an unlimited knowledge base, unlimited chatbots and unlimited support agents. Above that you are mostly buying ticket volume, translations and reports.

Annual billing saves 17% on every paid tier, and there is a 14-day trial. Re-read on the App Store listing on 29 August 2026; rating 4.5 from 243 reviews.

How to actually use HelpCenter

Start on free and build the FAQ page first: it is the part that earns its keep whether or not you ever use the ticketing, because it is indexable and deflects the questions that would otherwise become tickets. Connect email before chat — chat creates an expectation of response time that a one-person store cannot always meet, and an unanswered chat widget reads worse than no widget. If you sell in more than one language, price LangShop into the decision now rather than discovering the gap later.

Frequently asked questions

What should I weigh before buying Customer Support software?

Judge it on evidence rather than adjectives. Look for a published security page, single sign-on and role controls on the plan you would actually buy (they are often gated to higher tiers), a status page with real incident history, and a clear answer on where your data is stored. If the tool will hold anything sensitive, those four answers matter more than any feature comparison.
