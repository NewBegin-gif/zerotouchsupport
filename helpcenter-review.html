HelpCenter for Shopify: the meter is tickets, not agents

Most helpdesks charge you for people. HelpCenter charges you for work. That single difference decides whether it is the cheapest support app on your Shopify store or quietly one of the more expensive ones, and it is not something the pricing page puts in front of you.

What it is

HelpCenter is a Shopify-shaped helpdesk: email, live chat and Messenger land in one ticket queue, and alongside that sits an FAQ builder that publishes a real, indexable FAQ page on your storefront. It is free to install. The three things it replaces for a small store are a shared inbox, a chat widget and the FAQ page nobody ever gets around to writing.

Why it belongs in a zero-touch workflow

The FAQ builder is the part that does the zero-touch work, and it is easy to overlook because ticketing is the headline feature. An FAQ page answers the question before it becomes a ticket, and because it is indexable it keeps answering it while you sleep. Ticketing handles the conversations you could not avoid; the FAQ page reduces how many of those there are. If you only ever use half this app, use that half.

That ordering matters for the bill too, and this is where the app gets genuinely interesting. Connect email before you connect chat. A chat widget creates an expectation about response time, and an unanswered chat reads worse to a customer than no chat at all. A one-person store that switches chat on during a launch week and forgets it afterwards has made its support worse, not better.

The pricing, and the trap inside it

Read on the Shopify App Store listing on 29 August 2026. Free covers ten tickets a month and then charges $0.25 for each one after that. Basic is $5.99 a month, or $59.99 a year for a 17% saving, and covers fifty tickets, then $0.16 each. Standard is $19.99 a month or $199.99 a year for 300 tickets, then $0.15. Advanced is $49.99 a month or $498.96 a year for 1,000 tickets, then $0.10. Every paid tier carries a 14-day trial and all charges are in USD.

Notice what the meter is. Basic hands you unlimited support agents — so the usual SaaS instinct, keep the seat count down, buys you nothing here. What you are actually buying is ticket allowance, and the overage rate runs the opposite way to what most people expect: the more you pay upfront, the cheaper each extra ticket becomes.

Run the arithmetic before you pick a tier, because the headline price is misleading at surprisingly low volumes. At 200 tickets a month, Basic is $5.99 plus 150 overage tickets at $0.16 — about $30. Standard is a flat $19.99 and includes all 200. Standard wins well before you reach its ceiling. Push to 300 tickets and Basic works out at $45.99, because the 250 over the allowance add $40, against $19.99 for Standard covering the same 300 outright. The $5.99 entry price only holds if you genuinely stay under fifty tickets a month.

One dependency worth knowing

Translating your FAQs into other languages is handed off to a separate app, LangShop. If you sell in more than one language, that is two subscriptions rather than one, and it is better to price that in now than to discover it after you have built the FAQ page. For a single-language store it is a non-issue.

Who it is for

Best for small Shopify stores that want ticketing, live chat and a searchable FAQ page out of one app instead of bolting three together — and that will actually look up their own ticket volume before choosing a tier.

Skip it if you already run a general helpdesk like Zendesk or Freshdesk for a business wider than the store. HelpCenter is Shopify-shaped, not company-shaped, and running two helpdesks is worse than running the wrong one.

How we would start

Install on free and build the FAQ page first. It earns its keep whether or not you ever lean on the ticketing, because it deflects the questions that would otherwise arrive as tickets — and on the free tier, tickets past ten cost you $0.25 each. Add email next, chat only once you can answer it. Then, after a month of real volume, count your tickets and pick the tier from that number rather than from the feature list. On this app the feature steps matter less than the arithmetic; the one that genuinely changes what you can do is Free to Basic, which unlocks unlimited FAQs, an unlimited knowledge base, unlimited chatbots and unlimited agents.
