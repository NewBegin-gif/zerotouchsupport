respond.io Review (2026)

The entry plan is the inbox without the engine.

Best for

a small team that already lives in WhatsApp and Instagram DMs and has outgrown a shared phone — retail, clinics, travel, local services. Per-contact pricing suits you if headcount grows faster than conversation volume, and the seven-day trial without a card lets you check that before committing.

Skip it if

you only need one channel: a plain shared inbox or a helpdesk will cost less than an omnichannel platform you use a fifth of. Skip the Starter plan specifically if automation is the reason you are looking — you will be on Growth within a month. And if your monthly active contacts run into the tens of thousands, get the number in writing first, because that is the meter and the published ladder stops being informative.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

At $79 you get shared inboxes across WhatsApp, Instagram, Messenger, SMS and live chat, and that alone solves the “five people answering from one phone” problem. But workflow automation, broadcasts, AI Agents and the API — the things most people picture when they read “omnichannel platform” — begin at $159, so the real entry price for an automated setup is double the headline. Work out which side of that line you sit on before comparing it with anything else.

And note the meter: you pay per active contact per month, so cost follows how many people message you, not how many staff you add. That is kinder than per-seat pricing for a small team fielding a lot of chats, and harsher if you have a big list you talk to often.

What buyers report about respond.io

Hands-on: not tested by usReported by buyers: Capterra, read August 2026

Themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run respond.io ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Consolidating WhatsApp, Instagram, Messenger, email and live chat into one inbox is the reason people stay — named in review after review as the thing that stopped several people answering from one phone (Capterra, September 2025).
  • • The automation holds up at agency scale. A December 2025 reviewer running it across multiple outlets, business units and clients describes lead qualification, routing, follow-up and escalation logic that does not become fragile, and rates the CRM-style structure as rare in chat platforms.
  • • Cost-effective lead handling and the reporting, from a Botswana reseller running it for partner-network sales (Capterra, June 2026).
What they complain about
  • • It is not plug-and-play. The same reviewers who rate it highest say teams without clear SOPs overcomplicate it, integration takes technical know-how, and reports are limited to the platform's own templates rather than being customisable (Capterra, December 2025 and June 2026).
  • • Billing is the complaint that survives every platform. Reviewers report being billed while the platform was not in use (April 2026), a refund refused after downtime made the product unusable (March 2026), and being told they had to pay for the months they had not been subscribed before they could rejoin (July 2026).
  • • One reviewer describes long SMS messages being split into roughly 150-character chunks that arrive out of order or not at all, with support attributing it to the upstream carrier (Trustpilot, June 2026).
  • • Partner status does not help when Meta is the problem: a January 2026 reviewer says WhatsApp Business API issues went unresolved despite the official-partner badge.

Where the ratings disagree. The two platforms disagree more sharply than usual: 4.6 from 60 on Capterra against 2.0 from 16 on Trustpilot, where 81% are one star. Trustpilot's own page notes the company has not invited reviews recently, so what lands there is unprompted — and unprompted reviews of a subscription tool skew to billing and cancellation. (That profile is also filed under a different company name and carries an archival notice, which is a further reason to weigh it carefully.) The informative part is the overlap: the same account of a frozen account that kept being charged appears on both platforms in August 2025 under the same reviewer's name. Product praise is real and billing complaints are real; they are not in contradiction.

When the paid plan is worth it

The line between Starter and Growth is not volume, it is whether the product automates anything. Starter at $79 buys team and custom inboxes, the mobile app, AI Prompt, AI Assist, basic reports and two-factor authentication -- a shared inbox, in other words. Workflow automation, broadcasts, AI Agents, advanced reports, the developer API and the Zapier and Make integrations all begin at Growth, at $159. If you are buying this to stop doing something by hand, Growth is the entry price and Starter is not a cheaper version of it.

Advanced at $279 adds multiple workspaces, SSO, webhooks, HTTP requests inside workflows and phone-number masking. Worth noting that although a figure is printed, its button reads “Talk to Sales”, so treat it as an indication. Paying yearly takes about 20% off across the board: $948, $1,908 and $3,348 a year against $1,188, $2,388 and $4,188 paid monthly. One thing we could not resolve and you should ask about before signing.

The pricing page carries a selector for 1,000 / 2,500 / 5,000 / 10,000 / more than 10,000 monthly active contacts under the heading “pay only for the contacts you talk to” -- but the prices we read did not change between those settings. Monthly active contacts is the meter this product bills on, so get your volume quoted in writing rather than inferring it from the page. The trial is 7 days and takes no card.

How to actually use respond.io

Connecting the channel is the whole setup, and two prerequisites sit outside respond.io. Your Meta Business Manager profile has to list a real, reachable business website — the docs are blunt: "A legitimate, publicly accessible business website is mandatory in your Business Info — without one, your WABA may be disabled." And set the account currency to USD first, which the docs call "crucial to avoid issues with sending business-initiated conversations, even when you have sufficient credits." The path runs Workspace Settings, Channels, Add Channel, the WhatsApp Business Platform card, Connect with Facebook, pick or create the Business portfolio and WhatsApp Business Account, then add and OTP-verify a number.

Distilled from respond.io help centre, WhatsApp Business Platform (API) Quick Start — prerequisites, phone-number rules, Meta Business Manager requirements and the ten connection steps (respond.io/help/whatsapp/whatsapp-api-quick-start), read 20 August 2026.

Common respond.io setup mistakes

Where respond.io setups actually go wrong, and what it costs you when they do. Same sources as the setup notes above — not from a walkthrough we ran ourselves.

Watch out for the number.

  • It cannot already be registered on the WhatsApp personal or Business app — delete that account first — and Meta's free virtual number is "for testing purposes only": no approved display name, no Click-to-WhatsApp ads, no migration.
  • Choosing to add a number later "will prevent the Channel from being connected." If the final screen reads Continue to feature setup rather than Finish, you are not done.
