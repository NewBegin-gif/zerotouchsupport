eDesk: you pay per agent, then per AI resolution

Most helpdesks charge for seats and hand you the AI for free, or charge for the AI and hope you do not count. eDesk does something rarer: it charges for both, and it charges for the AI only when the AI actually finishes the job.

What it is

eDesk is a helpdesk built for people who sell in more than one place. Amazon, eBay, Shopify and email land in one inbox, with the order behind each message attached to it, so an agent answering "where is my parcel" is looking at the parcel rather than at another tab. That marketplace focus is the whole point of the product.

Why it belongs in a zero-touch workflow

The AI Agent is included on every paid tier, and it bills separately at $0.99 per automated resolution. That pricing is worth pausing on, because it is unusual and it is honest: you are charged when a query is actually resolved without a human, not when the bot has a go. A deflection tool that bills per attempt is incentivised to attempt; one that bills per outcome is incentivised to succeed.

It also makes the maths legible in a way most AI pricing is not. If an agent handles thirty tickets a day and costs you a fraction of that per hour, a resolution at 99 cents either clearly beats the human or clearly does not — and you can work out which without a spreadsheet.

The pricing

Re-verified 17 August 2026. There is a free plan to start. Essential is $39 per agent a month, Growth is $89 and marked recommended, Professional is $119, and Enterprise is a quote. Those are the annual figures: paying monthly is 20% more, so Essential becomes $49 rather than $39. On top of any paid tier, the AI Agent bills $0.99 per automated resolution.

So your bill has two independent dials: how many humans you employ, and how many queries the AI closes. They move in opposite directions if the tool works, which is the right shape for a support stack — but it does mean the invoice is never just the seat count, and a month with unusual volume shows up twice.

Who it is for

Best for e-commerce and marketplace sellers who want one unified inbox across Amazon, eBay, Shopify and email, with order data and AI-assisted replies where support and revenue overlap.

Skip it if you run a non-commerce SaaS or service business, where general helpdesks fit better and cost less — or if your ticket volume is low enough that a shared inbox already copes.

How we would start

Connect your busiest channel first, not all of them. The value of eDesk is the order data sitting next to the message, and you can judge that on one channel in a week. Then let the AI Agent run for a month and read the invoice line for resolutions as what it is: a count of tickets a human did not touch. Divide it by your own cost per ticket. If the answer is comfortable, add channels; if it is not, the problem is your ticket mix, not the pricing.
