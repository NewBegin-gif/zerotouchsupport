What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

The ‘callers can’t tell it’s AI’ claim is the vendor’s own: they offer a live demo number, so test that before you commit. One more thing to read carefully on the sibling product's page. LucroChat's ROI calculator reports its return “Based on Growth plan ($99/mo)”, while the plan table on the same page prices Growth at $59 a month (first read 24 August 2026, unchanged when we re-read it on 15 September 2026, and still there after the vendor told us that day it had been fixed).

The $99 is the higher of the two, so nobody is being lured with a low number, but a page that quotes two different prices for one plan is a page whose figures you check at checkout rather than take on trust.

What Lucrovox does

AI phone agent for local service businesses — answers missed calls, books jobs and captures leads 24/7 for trades like HVAC, plumbing and roofing.

When the paid plan is worth it

LucroVox publishes three flat tiers for the phone agent, Starter $199, Pro $499 and Enterprise $999 a month (read 24 August 2026), and what separates them is monthly call volume rather than a long feature list; above 2,000 calls the price is quoted. The Starter tier covers up to 100 AI-handled calls a month, which suits a business whose current inbound volume sits near or below that, since exceeding the cap is the concrete trigger to move up.

Pro, capped at 500 calls a month, also adds CRM integration and advanced lead qualification, features Starter does not include at all, so a business that wants call data flowing into an existing CRM needs Pro regardless of how many calls it actually takes. Enterprise raises the ceiling to 2,000 calls a month and adds white-label branding and multi-location support. What upgrading does not solve: any call the AI cannot fully resolve still gets transferred to a live person or left as a flagged message, on every tier.

A higher plan buys more automated call capacity, not less reliance on your own staff for complex or edge-case calls.

How to actually use Lucrovox

Setup starts with signing up for a plan: Starter and Pro carry a 14-day free trial, Enterprise a 30-day trial. You provide business details, a preferred call script and qualifying questions, and calendar access, connecting Google Calendar, Calendly, HousecallPro, ServiceTitan, Jobber, or another CRM, with anything else needing a custom API integration. No coding is required. LucroVox states most businesses go live within 48 hours of signing up, with billing on the 14-day plans starting on day 15.

The practical snag: CRM integration and advanced lead qualification sit behind the Pro tier, not Starter, so a business that signs up for Starter to test the waters and then finds it needs CRM sync has to upgrade mid-trial. Businesses running a system outside the listed integrations are pointed to a custom API build rather than a ready-made connector, which adds development work before the AI can actually book into that calendar.

Frequently asked questions

What does Lucrovox cost?

Our dated reading gives $199 a month for up to 100 AI-handled calls, $499 for up to 500 with CRM integration, and $999 for up to 2,000 with white-label and multi-location. On 17 August 2026 we could not re-confirm those from the page itself: it now leads with an ROI calculator rather than a plan table. Ask for the current tier list in writing.

What are the numbers on their page actually measuring?

Not price. The figures that dominate the page — $750 a week, $39,000 a year — come from a calculator that multiplies your missed calls by an assumed $500 job value and a 30% close rate. Those are defaults you can change, not measurements of your business, so treat the output as an illustration rather than a forecast.

What decides which tier I need?

Call volume: the tiers are bounded at roughly 100, 500 and 2,000 AI-handled calls a month. Count the calls you currently miss rather than the calls you receive, since that is the volume the service is meant to catch.

What should I weigh before buying Communication & Voice software?

Generally yes, since this category is built for it, but the details that bite are timezone handling, how per-seat cost scales as the team spreads, and whether numbers, routing or coverage exist in the countries your people actually sit in. Availability varies far more by country than the marketing pages suggest, so check your specific regions before committing.
