Someone has to answer: live receptionists against an AI agent

Researched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-01

The question is not human or AI. It is what your callers are actually calling about.

A missed call at a service business is often a lost job, which is why this category exists and why it is priced the way it is. Two of the options below put a real person on the line and bill you for their minutes; one puts an AI agent on the line and bills you per call it handles. The difference in cost per call is large.

What decides between them is not sophistication but content. If callers want opening hours, order status or a booking slot, a human is answering questions a system could answer. If they are choosing whether to trust you with their house or their case, the voice matters more than the answer.

How we picked

  • Prices read on the vendor’s own page and dated, with the unit named — receptionist-minutes, included minutes, or AI-handled calls.
  • We separate the two models rather than ranking across them, because they are priced an order of magnitude apart.
  • We say who should skip each one, including where voicemail-to-text is genuinely the right answer.

Sort your calls before you buy

Spend one week tagging incoming calls into three buckets: someone wants information, someone wants to book, someone is deciding whether to hire you. The first two are what an AI agent handles well and what a human service charges you a premium to do. The third is where a live receptionist earns the money.

Most businesses find the split is not what they assumed, and the answer is often both — an AI layer for hours and status, a human service for the calls that decide revenue. Pricing them as an either-or is what makes this category look expensive.

Frequently asked questions

Will callers notice it is an AI?

Often yes, and whether that matters depends entirely on why they rang. For a booking or an opening-hours question most people do not mind; for a decision about trusting you with something expensive, they do.

Is voicemail-to-text enough?

For low volume, frequently. The case for an answering service is that a caller who reaches voicemail often rings your competitor next — if that is not happening to you, this whole category is optional.

How do I compare minute-based and call-based pricing?

Work out your average call length and your monthly call count, then price both models against those two numbers. A short-call business is penalised by minute rounding; a long-call business is penalised by per-call pricing.
