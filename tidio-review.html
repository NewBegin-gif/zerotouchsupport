When the paid tier is worth it

The step that needs thinking about is not Starter to Growth, it is Growth to Plus. Growth at 59 a month month-to-month buys advanced analytics, user permissions, automatic assignment and macros against Starter's basic set, and it lifts the conversation cap from 100 to a ceiling the comparison table puts at 2,000.

That is a legible trade: you can count your conversations and decide. Plus is a different kind of purchase. It is 300 a month plus usage, and the usage rate is described as discounted but never printed.

The features you are buying are organisational rather than functional: departments, multiproject, custom branding, a dedicated success manager, wire transfer, custom seat counts. If you need departments and SSO you already know it.

If you are looking at Plus to escape a conversation cap, get the per-conversation rate in writing first, because on this plan that number decides the bill and it is the one figure the pricing page withholds. Premium is worth one sentence: it is where the 50 percent Lyro resolution guarantee and pay-per-resolution billing live.

That is the only place on the page where Tidio takes on any of the risk of the AI not working, and it is quoted by conversation with sales rather than by price.

How we would start

Install on the free plan and spend the first 50 Lyro conversations deliberately, because they are all you get. Point Lyro at your real FAQ and let it answer live traffic rather than testing it yourself, then read the transcripts: what you are measuring is how often it answers from your own data without guessing and how often it hands over.

That number, not the published 67 percent, is what a paid Lyro quota is worth to you. Size the meters before you pick a plan. Count the conversations your team actually handles in a month against Starter's 100 and Growth's 250-to-2,000 band, and count them separately from the Lyro quota, because the two are billed apart and a busy chat widget can exhaust the AI allowance while the help desk plan still has room.

If Flows matters to you, the third meter is visitors reached, not conversations. Then ask two questions in writing before you upgrade: what a billable conversation over the cap costs, and — if Plus is on the table — what the discounted usage rate per conversation is.

Neither is on the pricing page, and on the plans where they apply they decide the bill. Pay monthly for the first month or two even though it costs 20 percent more; the annual column is the cheaper one only if the tool survives its trial.

Who it is for

Best for support teams that want an AI agent and a help desk from the same vendor and can size all three meters — billable conversations, Lyro AI conversations and Flows visitors — against their own volume before they commit, and Shopify, WordPress or Wix stores that want to run live chat at zero and treat the 50 free Lyro conversations as a measurement of whether the AI answers their questions at all.

Skip it if you need the AI agent to keep answering past the first 50 conversations without paying, you cannot get the over-cap rate and the Plus usage rate quoted before signing, you want one per-seat price rather than three independent meters, or you are buying on the strength of the 67 percent resolution figure and need it as a commitment rather than an average.
