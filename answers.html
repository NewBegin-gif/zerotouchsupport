800.com

Is 800.com worth it?

800.com is genuinely best for businesses that want a professional toll-free or vanity number with call tracking, routing and analytics to look bigger and measure inbound calls. Skip it if you need a full unified-comms suite (video, chat, contact center), or you already have a phone system and just need occasional forwarding.

How much does 800.com cost?

(re-verified 2026-08-13, read on their own plans page with both billing tabs open) No free tier. On yearly billing: Startup $19/mo, Small Business $49/mo and Complete $99/mo; the same three month-to-month are $23, $59 and $119, badged SAVE 15%. Enterprise is quote-only. Startup covers 1,000 minutes, 1 number, 1 user and 1 extension; Small Business 3 numbers, 3 users and 3 extensions with minutes listed as unlimited subject to the Fair Usage Policy; Complete 1,000 minutes, 5 numbers (local, toll-free or vanity), unlimited users and extensions, 1,000 transcribe minutes and 500 AI Agent minutes. Every plan carries a 30-day money-back guarantee. Listings that still name Personal, Unlimited or Pro plans are out of date. Always verify the live price.

What is the catch with 800.com?

It does one thing — toll-free and vanity numbers with call tracking and routing — cleanly, but it's a phone-number layer, not a full business-comms platform, so you won't get team chat, video or a contact center here. The 1,000-minute cap on Startup matters for higher call volume, and the step up to Small Business more than doubles the bill. For a business that mainly wants a professional toll-free presence and call analytics, it's straightforward and fairly priced. The 2026 AI Agents (auto-answering, appointment booking, spam filtering) are the newest piece and sit in the bundled Complete plan, so treat them as young rather than proven.

Aircall

Is Aircall worth it?

Aircall is genuinely best for sales and support teams (3+ seats) that want a polished cloud phone system embedded in their CRM/helpdesk, with call routing, analytics and 250+ integrations. Skip it if you're a solo operator or duo (the 3-license minimum prices you out), or you need a full contact-center suite with omnichannel routing.

How much does Aircall cost?

(re-verified 2026-08-17 in the browser; the page served us euros from an EU address, so your own storefront may quote dollars) Essentials €30 per licence a month billed annually or €40 billed monthly; Professional €50 annually or €70 monthly; a Custom tier needs 25 licences or more. The floor that matters is the three-licence minimum, which Aircall states on the page as €90 a month billed annually or €120 billed monthly for Essentials, and €150 or €210 for Professional. Essentials includes 1,000 outbound domestic minutes per user per month; Professional makes domestic outbound unlimited and adds the Power Dialer, voicemail drop, Salesforce CTI and advanced analytics. AI Voice Agents are priced separately: every account gets 50 free AI minutes a month plus 100 extra at sign-up, then bundles of 500 minutes for €175 or 2,500 for €725, custom bundles from €0.19 a minute above 10,000, or pay-as-you-go at €0.49 a minute up to 2,500 minutes and €0.39 above that. Always verify the live price.

What is the catch with Aircall?

Two structural catches: the 3-seat minimum makes the real starting price triple the sticker, and the annual-vs-monthly gap is steep (Professional: $50 vs $70). 'Unlimited calling' covers listed regions — check yours — and AI features increasingly live in add-ons or higher tiers. It's polished and integration-rich; just price the seats you'll actually have, on the billing cycle you'll actually choose.

Answering Service Care

Is Answering Service Care worth it?

Answering Service Care is genuinely best for a small business that loses money when a call goes to voicemail — trades, clinics, law and property firms — and wants real people answering around the clock without hiring a receptionist. It suits you especially if your call volume is genuinely low and predictable, because the plans are priced in minutes and nothing is gated behind a higher tier. Skip it if your callers mostly want things a human does not need to do — order status, opening hours, a password reset — because you are paying per minute for a person to read them out. Skip it too if your volume is high and variable: at 500-plus minutes you are past the published ladder and into a custom quote, and per-minute billing makes a busy month expensive in a way a flat-rate provider would not.

How much does Answering Service Care cost?

(2026-08-12, read off their own pricing page, both billing toggles) Four plans, priced on included minutes. Billed monthly: Starter $40 with **zero** minutes included and $1.75 per minute after that; Small Business $179 for 100 minutes, then $1.70; Pro $329 for 250 minutes, then $1.60; Enterprise $639 for 500 minutes, then $1.35. Paying a year upfront takes about 10% off the monthly rate: $161, $296 and $575 for the three larger plans. There is also a $50 start-up fee, which appears only in the prose underneath the table and not on the plan cards. Every plan carries the same feature set — 24/7 cover, bilingual English/Spanish operators, call transfers, SMS and email relays, custom greetings and hold music, a client portal, usage reporting and the mobile app — so you are buying minutes, not capability, and there is no long-term contract. One caution about the page itself: the explanatory text below the table contradicts the table three times, quoting Pro at $319, Enterprise at $599 and Starter overage at $1.65. We have used the figures from the pricing table, which is the one the buttons act on. Always verify the live price.

What is the catch with Answering Service Care?

Do the arithmetic before you pick a tier, because their own page does not. Starter buys the line and nothing else: at $40 plus $1.75 a minute, it stays the cheapest option only up to roughly 79 minutes a month — past that the $179 Small Business plan costs less. The same crossover logic repeats: Small Business gives way to Pro at about 188 minutes, and Pro gives way to Enterprise at about 444. Note too that Small Business is the one tier where an included minute ($1.79 each, $179 for 100) costs more than an overage minute ($1.70), so there is no saving in buying that bundle for its own sake. And the second thing to price in is the $50 start-up fee, which is easy to miss where they have put it.

Our dollar reading of the same page on 23 July 2026 gave Growth $39, Pro $79 and Business $199 annually, so which currency and which figure you see depends on where Wati places you — check your own checkout before you budget. On top of all of it sit Meta's own per-conversation message fees, which Wati bills through at its own rate card. Always verify the live price.

What is the catch with Wati?

The plan price is the smallest part of the bill. Growth is capped at three users and you cannot buy a fourth at any price, so growing past three people forces the jump to Pro; from there extra seats are $24 each, and $69 each on Business. Automation triggers, AI credits, the Shopify link and every additional WhatsApp number are all separately metered, and Meta's per-conversation fees land on top of the lot. Model your real message volume and seat count before committing — that is where the money actually goes.

Skip it if you're outside healthcare/practice verticals, or you're a single small office where a simple VoIP line and a separate review tool cost far less.

Quote-based, not publicly listed (2026). And it's deeply tuned for healthcare and dental front-desks, so if you're outside those verticals, much of what you're paying for won't apply. For a busy practice, the phone-texting-payments-reviews bundle is genuinely powerful.

Zendesk

Is Zendesk worth it?

Zendesk is genuinely best for support teams past the point where a shared inbox works, who need routing, a help centre and reporting in one place and have the volume to justify per-agent pricing. Skip it if you are a small team handling support out of email — the entry tier is email and ticketing only, the features people buy Zendesk for start at $55 a seat and the Copilot AI is not available below $115; also skip it if your case volume is low enough that per-resolution AI metering is more admin than benefit.

How much does Zendesk cost?

(re-verified 2026-08-12, read on their own pricing page from two countries) Per agent per month on annual billing, US storefront: Support Team $19, Suite Team $55, Suite Professional $115. Suite Enterprise + Copilot is quote-only. The page geo-prices — read from Europe the same three tiers show €19, €55 and €115 — so check which storefront your quote came from. Annual billing carries a stated 20% discount and the monthly rate is never printed. Paid add-ons on top of the seat price: Copilot $50 per agent/month (€50), Workforce Engagement bundle $50 (€50), Contact Center $83 (€82). Copilot can be attached to Professional and higher only. AI agents are included in Suite and Support plans but metered by 'automated resolutions' beyond the plan allowance, and App Builder, Action Builder and Voice are billed on consumption above their own allowances. Always verify the live price.

What is the catch with Zendesk?

Three separate multipliers sit on top of the number you first see. The prices shown are annual-billed — the page states a 20% annual discount and never prints the monthly rate, so budgeting month-to-month means adding roughly a quarter yourself. Copilot, the AI the product is currently sold on, is a $50 per-agent add-on that their own page restricts to Professional and higher: the $19 and $55 tiers cannot have it at any price, and on the $115 Professional seat it is a 43% uplift. And the AI agents that are included are metered by automated resolutions — past the allowance you pay per resolution, so the bill moves with how well the AI works rather than with how many people you hire, with App Builder, Action Builder and Voice metered on consumption alongside it. Price the tier you need with the add-ons you actually want switched on, because the $19 entry and a working configuration are far apart.

ZenZap

Is ZenZap worth it?

ZenZap is genuinely best for small teams that run work over messy WhatsApp/personal chat today and want organized, professional chat with tasks — starting free. Skip it if your workflows depend on a deep integration ecosystem (Slack's moat), or you're already paid into Microsoft Teams via 365.

How much does ZenZap cost?

(price re-verified 2026-08-02 against the vendor's own pricing page) (2026) Free plan with unlimited users, chats and built-in to-dos; Pro about $3/user/mo (integrations, storage); Business+ ~$8/user/mo (roles, audit trails, SLAs); Enterprise custom. What is the catch with ZenZap?

The pricing genuinely undercuts Slack — the honest question is ecosystem: team chat lives or dies on integrations and habit, and ZenZap's app catalog is a fraction of Slack's, so teams glued to hundreds of integrations will feel the gap. For WhatsApp-refugee teams that mainly need organized channels, tasks and work/life separation, the free tier is a remarkably complete landing spot.
