Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 15 Sep 2026

Starter is $19 per user per month on annual billing and $25 month to month, Professional $30 and $40, Ultimate $42 and $55. Read the tab before the number: the grid opens on yearly, so the lower column is what most write-ups quote, and the toggle beside it advertises "Save 3 Months' Cost" -- paying annually is a third cheaper per user.

The $18 that a plain fetch kept printing for Starter was the placeholder sitting in the HTML before the pricing script runs, not a column any visitor is shown. Basic still costs nothing, but fair-usage note 8 lower on that page says it is free only for the first 6 months from signup, after which the account must move to Starter to keep working - so Basic is a long runway, not a permanent free tier.

Full pricing notes (3 more paragraphs)

Starter also states a MINIMUM OF 2 USERS and includes 1,000 calling minutes within US/CA plus 100 SMS; unlimited US/CA minutes start one step higher at Professional (500 SMS), which is also where call recording, AI voicemail transcription and multilingual IVR begin. Ultimate adds smart DID, multilevel, holiday and CRM-based VIP routing with 1,000 SMS. Two different Enterprise cards sit on this page and they state different floors: the Core one a minimum of 20 users, the call-centre 'New Enterprise' a minimum of 50, both quote-only with unlimited calling to 48 countries.

The call-centre 'Pro Suite' ran $32-48/user/mo annual when it was last read on 15 August 2026. 10-day free trial on the paid tiers. Add-ons are priced apart: AI Voice Agent $45/user/mo with calling billed separately at $0.25/min, AI Copilot $10/user/mo, a dashboard-user seat $15/user/mo, CNAM $100 one-time, SMS Services from $3/mo with a $20 one-time setup fee, Spam Watch $3 per number/mo for US and Canada, $100 per number for spam remediation, $239 for 1,000 UAE minutes and $0.12 per branded call.

Overages are in the notes: incoming minutes on standard numbers $0.02/min, incoming SMS $0.01 per message past fair use, outgoing calls to the same number capped at 10 per hour, and parallel calls capped at 30% of total call capacity. The currency selector prices per market instead of converting: the same grid read EUR 19/29/39 in euros on 15 August 2026, below the dollar column rather than above it.

Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the price did

We capture CallHippo’s own pricing page every month and keep the figures, so this is our record of the page — not a forecast, and not someone else’s summary.

CallHippo prices per currency rather than converting, and our readings show the currency moving more than the ladder. On 19 July 2026 the dollar grid read $19, $30 and $42 for Starter, Professional and Ultimate. On 15 August 2026 the same grid served from Europe read EUR 19, 29 and 39 - not a conversion of the dollar column, and below it rather than above. On 14 and 15 September 2026 Professional and Ultimate were unchanged at $30 and $42 across three months, but on 15 September the reading was finally done properly, and it changed the meaning: the grid opens on its YEARLY tab, so $19, $30 and $42 are annual-billing rates. The monthly tab the same day, same currency and region, showed $25, $40 and $55. The $18 a plain fetch kept printing for Starter turned out to be the placeholder in the HTML before the pricing script runs, so there was never a one-dollar gap to resolve. The change that costs more than a dollar is not in the amounts at all: Starter now carries a 2-seat minimum and 1,000 US/CA minutes where our August reading had unlimited minutes starting there, and fair-usage note 8 now caps the free Basic tier at six months from signup.

When the paid plan is worth it

Basic is a number-rental shell: you pay only for the numbers, and it carries click-to-dial, global connect, voicemail, business hours, a smart failover system, SMS/MMS and 2FA. It is free rather than a trial in the plan grid, but fair-usage note 8 on the same page caps it at six months from signup, after which the account has to upgrade to Starter to keep calling - so treat Basic as a long runway, not a permanent free tier.

Starter ($19 per user per month on annual billing, $25 month to month) is NOT where unlimited calling begins: it buys 1,000 US/CA minutes and 100 SMS, and it will not sell to a single user, since the card states a minimum of 2 seats. Unlimited US/CA minutes arrive at Professional ($30 per user per month on annual billing, $40 month to month, 500 SMS), the same step that adds call recording, AI voicemail transcription and multilingual IVR - so for most teams that is the real first threshold, not Starter.

Ultimate at $42 annual, $55 month to month, buys routing depth (multilevel IVR, smart DID, holiday routing, CRM-based VIP routing, 1,000 SMS), which pays back only once there are queues to route. Enterprise is quote-only and the page states two different floors, 20 users on the Core card and 50 on the call-centre one. What no upgrade fixes: 'unlimited' is unlimited within the number region you select, so international destinations still meter; incoming minutes past the free pool cost $0.02 and incoming SMS past fair use $0.01; outgoing calls to the same number are capped at 10 per hour on every tier; and the WhatsApp Business API is sold on the call-centre suite rather than unlocked by a higher phone-system plan.

The add-ons are where a quoted seat price quietly doubles - copilot, voice agent, dashboard seats, CNAM and spam monitoring are all billed on top.

How to actually use CallHippo

A signup opens a 10-day free trial with no credit card, but nothing dials until a number is attached. Numbers are bought inside the app — Numbers, then Add Number, choose a country, filter, upload documents, pay — and the documents are where the delay lives. US local and Australia mobile numbers are the only ones CallHippo lists as available without submitting documentation; everything else needs country-specific paperwork, with UK local numbers requiring UK-issued documents.

Support states numbers typically activate within 24 to 48 business hours after document verification, and that regulator rules allow no exceptions, so a new account can sit unusable while paperwork clears. Each user includes 1 free standard number; premium numbers are chargeable. Texting is a slower, separate track: SMS services carry a one-time setup fee of $20 per account and a documented approval window of 2 to 4 weeks, so US business texting has to be started well before launch rather than switched on.

Frequently asked questions

Will a communication tool work for a remote or distributed team?

For a phone system the team can sit anywhere; the details that bite are routing across time zones, how per-seat cost scales as the team spreads, and whether local or toll-free numbers, porting and call routing exist in the countries your people actually sit in. Number availability and calling rates vary far more by country than the marketing pages suggest, so check your specific regions before committing.
