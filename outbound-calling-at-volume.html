Calling at volume: dialer, call centre or just a good phone system

Researched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-01

Three of these are priced per licence and one has a minimum that quietly rules out small teams.

The tools here sit on a line from ‘phone number with analytics’ to ‘predictive dialer with compliance tooling’, and the price follows that line closely. Buying too far along it is the common mistake: a predictive dialer bought for a two-person team is expensive software solving a problem that team does not have.

Seat minimums matter as much as seat prices in this category. Two of these products are explicitly sized for teams, and one enforces that with a licence floor that makes it a non-option for a solo operator regardless of budget.

How we picked

  • Per-licence prices read on the vendor’s own page and dated, on annual billing where that is the default.
  • We state seat minimums, because they disqualify more buyers than price does.
  • We place each tool on the line from number to dialer, so the comparison is between products doing the same job.

Match the tool to the motion, not the headcount

Outbound volume, not team size, is what should decide how far along this line you buy. A three-person team working a large list may genuinely need dialer speed; a ten-person team making considered calls does not, and will resent software built to keep them dialling.

Check the compliance side before the feature side if you are calling consumers at volume. The rules on consent, timing and recording are not optional, and the tools that handle them properly cost more for exactly that reason.

Frequently asked questions

What is a predictive dialer for?

Keeping agents talking rather than waiting — it dials ahead and connects answered calls. That is valuable at high volume and pointless below it, where it mostly introduces awkward pauses for the person who picked up.

Do seat minimums really matter?

They are frequently the deciding factor for small teams. A three-licence floor on a $30 seat is a $90 monthly commitment whatever your headcount, which changes the comparison entirely.

Is a toll-free number still worth having?

For inbound credibility and call tracking, often yes. It is a presentation and measurement decision rather than a telephony one, and it does not replace a phone system.
