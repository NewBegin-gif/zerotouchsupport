Virtual phone systems: the seat price is not the bill

Researched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-01

All three are cheap per seat. What separates them is what happens to the minutes.

Cloud phone systems advertise a per-user price and bill you for two things: that seat, and the calling. The seat is the number on the pricing page, and it is rarely the number that matters. If your calling is international or high-volume, metered minutes and number rental will comfortably exceed the subscription.

The three tools here sit close together on seat price, which makes them easy to confuse and easy to choose badly between. The differences that actually show up on an invoice are the billing period, what the entry tier leaves out, and how the countries you call are priced.

How we picked

  • Seat prices are read on the vendor’s own page and dated, with the monthly and annual figures given separately where they differ.
  • We flag where a free or entry tier exists, and what it leaves out.
  • We say who should skip each one, since at this price level the wrong fit costs more in workarounds than in subscription.

Price the calling, not the seats

Take last month’s call log, split it by destination country, and price that against each vendor’s rates before you look at the subscription at all. For most teams making international calls the minutes are the larger half of the bill, and the seat price differences between these three are small enough to disappear inside that.

Then check the billing period. Two of the three quote annual rates that sit well below their monthly ones, so a monthly-vs-annual mix-up can make the wrong tool look cheaper by a wide margin.

Frequently asked questions

Is a free tier enough to run support on?

For one person testing whether a virtual number works, yes. For a team sharing an inbox and needing routing, no — the free tiers in this category are there to get you calling, not to run a desk.

Why do annual and monthly prices differ so much?

Because annual billing is worth a lot to the vendor and they price it accordingly. It is a real discount, but it is also a year of commitment to a tool you have not yet run in production.

What decides the real monthly cost?

Destination minutes and number rental, in most cases. Two teams on the same plan with the same headcount can see very different invoices purely on where they call.
