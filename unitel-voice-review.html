UniTel Voice Review (2026)Extra Users Cost $9.99

Communication & VoiceResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-21

UniTel Voice is a virtual phone layer, not a full unified-comms suite — you won't find the deep team messaging, video meetings and contact-center features pricier platforms bundle.

Best for: solopreneurs and small teams that want a professional toll-free or local number, auto-attendant and call routing on their existing phones — no hardware.
Who should skip it: if you need integrated video meetings, team chat and a full contact center, or you have no need for a separate business line at all.

Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 9 Sep 2026

Three plans, no contracts: Solo $9.99/mo, Team $19.99/mo and Office $29.99/mo, each with one phone number and unlimited minutes. Annual billing takes 30% off, making them $6.99, $13.99 and $20.99 a month. Solo is one user, Team carries unlimited users, and Office includes three with additional users at $9.99 each a month. The plan set we recorded in August — Start-Up, Unlimited at $24.99 and Office-Max at $69.99 — is no longer offered on the page.

Support is US-based, there is a 30-day money-back guarantee, and every plan includes a professionally recorded greeting. One caveat on those unlimited minutes: the pricing FAQ offers 3.9¢ per minute above "the minutes on your plan" and still names an Office-Max plan the page no longer sells, so the page contradicts its own cards — probably stale text, but unconfirmed. Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

Office charges per head; Team does not

Three plans and no contracts: Solo $9.99 a month, Team $19.99 and Office $29.99, each with one phone number and unlimited minutes. The seat model decides this, and it runs the opposite way to the price ladder. Team costs $19.99 and carries unlimited users. Office costs $29.99, includes three, and charges $9.99 for every additional person. The two cross almost immediately: at four users Office is $39.98 against Team's $19.99, and at ten it is $99.92 against the same $19.99 — five times the price for the same unlimited minutes.

What that buys is narrow, and their FAQ states it plainly: besides users and minutes, the only difference is that Office lets a user sync a softphone or a desk IP-phone to their extension, and it adds presence and team messaging. So the question is not how big your team is but whether anyone needs a physical desk phone or a desktop softphone. If calls land on mobiles and forwarded numbers, Team is the plan and Office is an expensive way to buy nothing.

One caveat on the annual discount: the page says extras added after an annual term starts are billed monthly, so add-on users do not get the 30% unless you prepay them through support. One thing the page does not settle. All three cards read "Unlimited Minutes", but the FAQ answer to "What happens if I go over my plan's minutes?" offers to "simply pay 3.9¢ per minute for additional minutes on any plan".

The same FAQ still points at an "Office-Max" plan the page no longer sells, so this reads like leftover text from the older metered line-up rather than a hidden cap. Either way the page contradicts its own cards, and if call volume is the reason you are buying, get the unlimited claim confirmed in writing before you commit to a year.

How to actually use UniTel Voice

Count your users first, because the seat model decides this and not the price ladder. Team is $19.99 a month and carries unlimited users. Office is $29.99, includes three, and charges $9.99 for each person after that — so four people on Office is $39.98 and ten is $99.92, against $19.99 on Team either way. Then check whether anyone actually needs what Office adds. Their FAQ is explicit: besides users and minutes, the only difference is that Office lets a user sync a softphone or a desk IP-phone to their extension, plus presence and team messaging.

If your calls land on mobiles and forwarded numbers, that is nothing you will use. Use the 30-day money-back window to test the thing that actually matters: call quality and the auto-attendant, on the devices and networks your team really uses. There are no contracts, so annual billing at 30% off is a straightforward saving once you know the calls hold up — take it in month two rather than month one.

One footnote: extras added after an annual term starts are billed monthly, so added seats do not get the discount.

Distilled from www.unitelvoice.com/pricing read on 9 September 2026 — the three plan cards (Solo, Team, Office) in both the monthly and the annual billing state, the per-plan price values the page carries for each state, and the pricing FAQ, including the answers on what separates the plans, on adding users to the Office plan at $9.99 each, and on how annual billing treats services added later.

The natural comparison is Grasshopper or Google Voice — Grasshopper is a close, pricier equivalent; Google Voice is cheaper but barer — UniTel's Team tier, unlimited users at $19.99, is the value play.

Frequently asked questions

What does UniTel Voice cost?

$9.99 a month for Solo, $19.99 for Team and $29.99 for Office, or $6.99, $13.99 and $20.99 on annual billing, which takes 30% off. Every plan carries one phone number and unlimited minutes, with a 30-day money-back guarantee and US-based support. Read on their own pricing page on 9 September 2026 — the Start-Up, Unlimited and Office-Max plans our August capture recorded are no longer offered.

What does it cost for a team rather than one person?

The plan price is not the team price. Additional users on the Office plan are $9.99 per user per month on top of the plan fee, and you can add them at any time after signing up. For a five-person office that is the plan plus four extra seats, so work from the seat count rather than the headline.

Are the minutes really unlimited?

The three plan cards all say "Unlimited Minutes", and that is what you are buying. Their pricing FAQ muddies it: asked what happens if you go over the minutes on your plan, it answers that you can "simply pay 3.9¢ per minute for additional minutes on any plan". That same FAQ still refers to an Office-Max plan the page no longer sells, so it looks like text left over from their older metered plans — but they have not cleaned it up, so ask support to confirm before you prepay for a year.

Is there really no contract?

That is what the page states, alongside a 30-day money-back guarantee — if you are not happy within thirty days they refund without questions. Annual billing is optional and is what buys the 30%-plus discount, so the trade is the usual one: commit for a year to pay less, or stay monthly and keep the exit.

What should I weigh before buying Communication & Voice software?

Generally yes, since this category is built for it, but the details that bite are timezone handling, how per-seat cost scales as the team spreads, and whether numbers, routing or coverage exist in the countries your people actually sit in. Availability varies far more by country than the marketing pages suggest, so check your specific regions before committing.
