We tested it · 2026-11-13

On 13 November 2026 we tested Zendesk Suite Professional on its trial, using our own site as the knowledge source. We built a Billing group and a routing trigger with the admin copilot, saved one synthetic invoice ticket, and asked the AI agent a Dutch and an English question. We did not open checkout, and we did not test support, invitations, roles, API access, export or cancellation.

How we tested

  1. Read the accelerated setup and the trial banner
  2. Asked the admin copilot to create a Billing group and a trigger, approving each step
  3. Saved one synthetic ticket as an internal note and read the assignee field
  4. Asked the AI agent one English and one Dutch question
  5. Read the annual and monthly plan comparison
  • Daan created the account the day before and we did not see that step. In the accelerated setup the website address came prefilled, and about 35 seconds later the AI agent had read our site. The setup asked for no card, no phone number and no mailbox connection. An optional banner offered an expert call.
  • The trial runs on Suite Professional with 12 of 14 days left when we looked. The trial therefore includes features that the entry plan Support Team does not.
  • The admin copilot created the Billing group on the first approval. The routing trigger took three approvals: the copilot first used the field subject, which Zendesk rejected, then title, then subject_includes_word. The whole job took about 1.5 minutes.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Copilot, the AI the product is currently sold on, is a $50 per-agent add-on that their own page restricts to Professional and higher: the $19 and $55 tiers cannot have it at any price, and on the $115 Professional seat it is a 43% uplift. And the AI agents that are included are metered by automated resolutions: past the allowance you pay per resolution, so the bill moves with how well the AI works rather than with how many people you hire.

App Builder, Action Builder and Voice are metered on consumption alongside it. Price the tier you need with the add-ons you actually want switched on, because the $19 entry and a working configuration are far apart.

What buyers report about Zendesk

We tested Zendesk ourselves on 2026-11-13, but one test is a narrow sample, so here is what other buyers report. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • For a large or multi-department support operation the depth, ecosystem and enterprise controls are the safest bet in the category.
  • • The integration marketplace is the largest in support software.
  • • On SourceForge, an administrator credits the team with fast response times and actually solving the problem, and a startup customer notes that entry credit keeps the cost manageable in the early growth phase.
What they complain about
  • • All Suite plans are annual contracts. There is no month-to-month at standard rates, and Zendesk's own FAQ confirms cancellations and downgrades get no refund.
  • • The cost problem is not the starting plan, it is what happens after rollout. Copilot and Workforce Engagement together add roughly 87% to the seat price, and the AI resolution meter bills on top of that.
  • • The native AI deflects more than it resolves: real-world resolution runs about 10-20%.
  • • Setup takes longer and needs more technical skill than the demos suggest.
  • • Non-enterprise customers report slow, automated and inconsistent support: from a company selling support software.
  • • A CEO on SourceForge calls the pricing structure opaque, every additional capability turns out to be another paid add-on, and another reviewer there judges the suite expensive for what the entry level connects you to.
  • • A SourceForge reviewer of eighteen months describes forwarded Gmail simply stopping, Zendesk declining to support their spam filter, and no resolution being offered; they moved to another vendor. It is one account, but it is the churn story in miniature.
  • • Among the Trustpilot reviews that come from Zendesk's own customers (2026), the complaints are a slow, bandwidth-heavy interface that gets worse over the years, support that answers slowly or only when seats are being added, and an account manager who left without a handover just before renewal, with an AI bot handling the cancellation request.

Where the ratings disagree. SourceForge (read 12 August 2026) does not contradict the others: the price-opacity and add-on complaints match what G2 and Capterra reviewers raise. What it does not contain is anything about contract terms or AI-resolution billing, so the buying-stage friction has to be read off Zendesk's own pricing page rather than off reviews. TrustRadius and Gartner Peer Insights both refused our requests, so they are absent here rather than checked and dismissed. Trustpilot shows 738 reviews with 80% at one star (read 7 October 2026), but much of that comes from consumers trying to reach a company that runs its support on Zendesk, not from people who bought Zendesk. Read it for the few buyer reviews, not for the score.

The $19 tier cannot have the AI at any price

The ladder above is the number you first see. Three separate multipliers sit on top of it. The first is billing period. Those prices are annual-billed. Since September the page also prints the monthly rate: read from the Netherlands on 7 October 2026 it is €25, €69 and €149 per agent against €19, €55 and €115 annual. The page calls that a 20% annual discount; worked out per tier the saving is 24%, 20% and 23%.

The US storefront could not be read from here, so its monthly dollar rates are not confirmed. The second is Copilot, and it is the important one. Copilot is a $50 per-agent-per-month add-on, and their own page restricts it to Professional and higher. So the $19 and $55 tiers cannot have the AI this product is currently sold on, at any price, and on the $115 Professional seat it is a 43% uplift.

A ten-agent team wanting the advertised product is on $165 a seat, or $19,800 a year. Workforce Engagement is another $50 and Contact Center $83. The third is metering. The AI agents that ARE included in Suite and Support plans are counted by 'automated resolutions' beyond the plan allowance. The page now states that allowance: 5 resolutions per agent a month on Support Team and Suite Team, 10 on Suite Professional, then €1.50 each committed or €2.00 pay-as-you-go (Netherlands storefront, 7 October 2026).

That means the bill moves with how well the AI performs rather than with how many people you employ. It is an unusual shape, and one that rewards success with an invoice. App Builder, Action Builder and Voice are billed on consumption above their own allowances as well. So price the tier you need with the add-ons you would actually switch on, and compare that figure. The distance between the $19 entry and a working configuration is the whole story here, and no competitor comparison based on the entry price means anything.

    How to actually use Zendesk

    Decide whether you need Copilot before you look at tiers, because it settles the plan for you. Copilot is a $50 per-agent add-on and Zendesk's own page restricts it to Professional and higher, so if the AI is why you are here, the $19 and $55 tiers are not cheaper options, they are unavailable ones. A ten-agent team on Professional with Copilot is $165 a seat, or $19,800 a year.

    Check which storefront you are quoting from. The page geo-prices, and the same three tiers read $19/$55/$115 in the US and €19/€55/€115 from Europe, which are not the same money. Make sure your quote and your comparison come from the same one. Budget the billing period properly: the headline prices are annual-billed, and month-to-month costs more. From the Netherlands on 7 October 2026 the page printed €25, €69 and €149 per agent monthly against €19, €55 and €115 annual.

    Then estimate automated resolutions, not just seats. The included AI agents are metered by resolution beyond your allowance, so your bill grows as the AI succeeds. The page now prints both numbers: 5 resolutions per agent a month on the Team tiers and 10 on Professional, then €1.50 each committed or €2.00 pay-as-you-go (Netherlands storefront, 7 October 2026). Ask the same for App Builder, Action Builder and Voice, which are all metered on consumption.

    What does Zendesk cost once the AI is switched on?

    More than the tier you were quoted, and not at every tier. Read on 12 August 2026: Copilot is a $50 per agent per month add-on and their page restricts it to Professional and higher, so the $19 Support Team and $55 Suite Team seats cannot have it at any price. On the $115 Professional seat it is a 43% uplift, taking a working AI configuration to $165 per agent per month before the AI agents themselves are metered.

    Why does the price I see differ from the price you quote?

    Because the page geo-prices. Reading zendesk.com/pricing from two countries on 12 August 2026 gave $19, $55 and $115 per agent per month on the US storefront and €19, €55 and €115 on the European one: the same digits in a different currency, which is not the same money. The add-ons are not even numerically parallel: Contact Center reads $83 in dollars and €82 in euros. Check which storefront your quote came from.

    What else gets billed beyond the seat price?

    Two meters run on top of the per-agent figure. AI agents are included in Suite and Support plans but billed by 'automated resolutions', Zendesk's own definition is that you pay per customer request the AI successfully resolved, so past your plan's allowance the bill rises with how well the AI performs, not with headcount. Separately, their page lists App Builder, Action Builder and Voice as consumption-billed above their allowances. The headline figures are annual-billed. Since September the page also prints monthly rates: €25, €69 and €149 per agent from the Netherlands on 7 October 2026.

    Can an agent who is not an admin see their own performance report?

    Partly, and it depends on the plan. Zendesk's help article 'Giving users access to Explore' (read 7 October 2026) says that on the Team plans, which come with Explore Lite, every Support user gets view-only access to Zendesk's prebuilt dashboards and nobody can build custom reports. On Suite Professional, which comes with Explore Professional, ordinary agents have no access to reporting at all until an admin grants them Viewer or Editor. Whether a dashboard can be limited to one agent's own numbers is not confirmed in these articles.

    Can agents see a customer's Shopify orders inside the ticket?

    Yes, on every Suite plan; a standalone Support plan also needs Chat. Zendesk's articles on setting up and using the Shopify integration (read 7 October 2026) say the app shows the customer's Shopify orders in the ticket and lets agents refund or cancel an order there. Email tickets can be assigned to one agent or a group on every plan, but not to light agents.

    What can I take with me if I leave Zendesk?

    Zendesk's export article (read 7 October 2026) says the built-in export to JSON, CSV or XML is not available on Team plans, only on Suite Growth and up and Support Professional and up. It is switched off by default: the account owner has to ask Zendesk Support to enable it, and only admins, or custom roles with export permission, can run it. The REST API works on every plan, and AI agent tickets cannot be exported. After you cancel, Zendesk deletes the data after 90 days. To export inside that window you ask Zendesk for a temporary reactivation.
