We tested it · 2026-11-17

The app was installed on our Shopify development store, which we set up, and our AI assistant ran the test in our browser. On 17 November 2026 the session tested AskNest AI on a Shopify development store on the Free plan: the install, a product sync of 18 items, the widget switched on in the theme editor preview (not saved), a best-sellers question that gave four product cards although the listing puts best-seller suggestions on Starter, a return question that got no details both before and after a policy page was synced (the text of that page was not looked at), the plan cards and the Starter approval screen (not approved). The first answer came after about 8 minutes and 5 steps. Nothing was paid, and the app was uninstalled afterwards. Install: the App Store listing (English) was read first; at 18:00 the install form was submitted in our own tab and the Shopify dialog Install app asked for View customer data (device and activity data), View staff and contributor data (store owner, blog contributors) and View store data (products, orders, Online Store, Shopify admin); Install was clicked. The app opened at once, with no separate account, no password and no e-mail address needed, on a dashboard with Current plan: Free and App: Inactive (Activate). The setup guide showed Total FAQs 0, Active FAQs 0, Synced products 0, Synced policies 0 and Replies used 0. The admin was in English. Core action, a shopper question answered in the widget: Sync products on the Products page gave, after about 5 seconds, Synced products 18 (Available 16, Unavailable 2, Vendors 6, Product types 4). In the theme editor, under App embeds, AskNest AI Widget was switched on and a button Ask AI appeared in the preview; this theme change was NOT saved. The widget says Hi! Ask me about store FAQs, products, shipping, returns, or product recommendations. With two chips, Show me best sellers and What is your return policy? And the line Answers use this store's saved FAQs and synced product data. Question 1, with no policy and no FAQs: What is your return policy? The answer said that the bot does not have the return details available and asked the shopper to contact the shop by e-mail (the address of the shop was in the answer and is hidden in the images). Question 2: Show me best sellers on the Free plan gave four product cards with View product, The Multi-managed Snowboard, Selling Plans Ski Wax, The Collection Snowboard: Hydrogen and The Videographer Snowboard, in about 6 seconds. The listing says best-seller suggestions are on Starter and up, so this card list appeared on a plan where the listing says it is not included. Policy sync: Sync policies on the Knowledge page gave 1 policy page synced successfully., Policies synced 1 and Answer priority Hybrid (FAQs first, product-specific data second, policy pages third, then safe AI fallback). What text the synced policy page held was not looked at. The same return question after the sync again gave the answer that no details were available and to contact the shop by e-mail. So with one policy page synced the bot still could not answer the return question, but because the policy text was not checked it is not known whether the page was empty. Time: the first answer came at about 18:08, about 8 minutes and 5 steps after the install (install, sync products, theme editor, widget on and open, ask a question); the policy sync and the FAQ entry are not in that count. Plans: in the app, Current plan: Free and Shopify billing: No paid subscription; Free $0 a month with 50 AI replies a month, AI FAQ answers, Product finder widget, Policy page answers, Storefront chat widget and Usage tracking; Starter $4.99 with 500 AI replies a month, Best-seller suggestions, Product recommendations and an e-mail fallback; Growth $9.99 with 1,500 replies; Pro $19.99 with 5,000 replies. The listing has the same four prices and says AI replies in up to 3 shopper languages on Starter and in all supported languages on Growth and Pro; the app shows no language difference per plan. Checkout: Upgrade to Starter opened the Shopify screen Approve subscription: AskNest AI by Ecommerce Storez, plan AskNest AI Starter, $4,99 USD every 30 days, Subtotal $4,99 plus any applicable taxes, Total $4,99 Due 17 Nov, a row Add payment method and a greyed out Approve button. There is no free trial. The sentence about a test charge was NOT on the screen and a payment method had to be added, so nothing was approved; no card was entered; the screen was closed with Cancel. Cancelling: on the Free plan there is no subscription. For a paid plan the app says that downgrading to Free cancels the active Shopify subscription; that was not tried because no subscription existed. Removing the app goes through Settings, Apps, the menu of the app and Uninstall; the dialog says that you can no longer use the app, that any data managed by the app, such as discounts, will be deleted, and that a request goes to the app developer within 48 hours to delete all personal customer information; a reason is required (we chose Testing multiple apps). Not tested: own FAQs and a return question answered from them, the text of the synced policy page, the languages (one chosen language on Free, detection on Starter), hand over to a person, questions outside the FAQs after the limit of 50 replies, reply 51, the real shop (it has a password), a paid plan, any other question than the two chips, and a question about shipping. Pricing (2026)

✓ Pricing re-verified 19 Nov 2026

Four tiers, priced by AI replies per month, and the cheapest ladder in this category. Free: 50 AI replies a month, replies in one language you select, answers drawn from your FAQs, policy pages and product data, translated storefront widget, product finder and usage tracking. Starter $4.99 a month: 500 replies, automatic shopper-language detection across up to three languages, translated replies, product recommendations and best sellers.

Growth $9.99 a month: 1,500 replies and all supported languages. Pro $19.99 a month: 5,000 replies. All charges are billed in USD, and the listing says recurring and usage-based charges are billed every 30 days, and no paid tier shows a free trial, the free plan is the trial. What the listing never says is what happens when you run out: there is no overage rate anywhere on the page, so whether reply 501 costs money or simply does not happen is not published. 'Any language' is also not on offer; customer-facing replies and widget labels cover English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Dutch and Brazilian Portuguese, seven.

Developer Ecommerce Storez, 1255 23rd St NW, Washington DC; the app launched 8 May 2026 and has exactly one App Store review. Plans change, so check the live price on their site before you buy.

The catch

That is five stars, 16 July 2026, from an Austrian store, and it is about support responsiveness: they wanted the widget's language changed and someone called Tracy did it within minutes. That is a pleasant data point about the vendor and no data point at all about whether the bot answers correctly under load. The developer is a Shopify agency, not a support-software company. Ecommerce Storez sells Shopify themes, store builds and SEO audits from ecommercestorez.us, and AskNest is one of two apps it has shipped in 2026: the other, AgentNest, has no reviews.

AskNest has no product site of its own; the App Store listing is the entire published record, which is why the link we point at is the developer's agency site. And be clear about what this is not. There is no live human takeover on any tier, no ticketing, no inbox. It reads your FAQs, your policy pages and your catalogue and answers from them in seven languages. When a shopper asks something your policy pages do not cover, nothing on the listing describes what happens next: no handover to email, no escalation.

For a first line of defence on repetitive questions that is fine and five dollars is a fair price for it. As your only support channel it is not enough.

When the paid plan is worth it

At these prices the tier question is almost trivial and the interesting line is elsewhere. Fifty free replies is roughly two a day, which is a test rather than a service; $4.99 for 500 is the first plan that can actually sit on a live storefront. From there the ladder is close to linear: 500, 1,500, 5,000 replies for $4.99, $9.99, $19.99. Climbing it costs about a cent a reply at the bottom and under half a cent at the top, and picking wrong costs you five or ten dollars, not five hundred.

The one gate that is not about volume sits between Free and Starter: automatic shopper-language detection. On Free you choose a single reply language and every shopper gets it. Starter detects the shopper's language across three; Growth opens all seven. If your traffic is one country, that gate is worth nothing to you and the free tier may be enough. If you sell into Germany, France and the Netherlands from one storefront, it is the entire reason to pay, and it arrives for five dollars.

What you cannot price is the overage. No tier states what happens after the last reply: whether the widget goes quiet, queues, or bills. On a support tool that failure mode matters more than the tier, because the month you exceed your allowance is by definition the month shoppers were asking the most.

    How to actually use AskNest

    Write the FAQs before you install anything. This bot answers from your saved FAQs, your Shopify policy pages and your catalogue, so the quality of its answers is a direct function of how precise those three sources are. Install it against vague policy pages and you get vague answers with a confident tone. Then spend the 50 free replies deliberately. Ask it your ten most common questions in each language you sell in, and check that product names, prices and links come back intact: preserving those is a feature the vendor names explicitly, and therefore one worth verifying.

    Before you pay, ask support in writing what happens when the monthly reply cap is reached, because the listing does not say. And decide up front where a shopper goes when the bot cannot help: there is no human takeover in this app, so that route has to be your own contact page, visible in the widget.

    What does AskNest cost?

    Four tiers metered on AI replies a month, re-read on apps.shopify.com/asknest-ai-faq-product-bot on 19 September and 28 September 2026 and unchanged since 19 August. Free gives 50 replies a month. Starter is $4.99 for 500, Growth $9.99 for 1,500, Pro $19.99 for 5,000. Charges are billed in USD every 30 days. No paid tier advertises a trial, which makes sense here: the free plan is the trial, and at these amounts picking the wrong rung costs five or ten dollars rather than five hundred.

    What happens when I use up my monthly replies?

    The listing does not say, on any tier. There is no overage rate published anywhere on the page, so whether reply 501 costs money or simply does not arrive is unknown until it happens, and on a storefront widget, "does not arrive" means a shopper gets silence. Ask support in writing before you rely on it. It matters most at the free end: 50 replies a month is roughly two a day, so a single busy afternoon can exhaust a month.

    Which languages does it really support?

    Seven, and "any language" is not on offer: English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Dutch and Brazilian Portuguese for customer-facing replies and widget labels. How you get at them differs by tier. On Free you pick one reply language and every shopper gets that one. Starter detects the shopper’s own language across up to three, and Growth and Pro open all supported ones. So the step from free to $4.99 is not about volume at all; it is automatic language detection, which is worth nothing if your traffic is one country and is the whole reason to pay if you sell into several.

    Can a human take over when the bot gets stuck?

    No, not on any tier. There is no live handover, no ticketing and no shared inbox in this app. It reads your saved FAQs, your Shopify policy pages and your catalogue and answers from those, so when a shopper asks something outside them, the conversation ends where the widget ends. Decide up front where that shopper goes next and make the route visible in the widget itself; your own contact page has to carry what the app will not.
