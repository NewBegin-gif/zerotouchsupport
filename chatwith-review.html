We tested it · 2026-10-21

Tested by our team on 21 October 2026 on a new account, without connecting a website or paying: Daan created the account and our AI assistant ran the test in Daan's browser. We trained the agent on 177 characters of text about a made-up shop (the same facts as in our Tidio test), then asked five fact questions, five rephrased ones and two it could not know.

  • Onboarding has four steps: Train, Brand, Test and Install. Training sources are a website (with linked pages on the same domain, or a sitemap), files, text or YouTube, against a counter of '0 / 500K characters'. Our 177 characters of text were trained in about 10 to 20 seconds.
  • The dashboard only opens after the onboarding: going to the app sent us back to the get-started steps. The first sign-in magic link returned 'INVALID_TOKEN'; a second one worked.
  • The guardrails slider starts on 'Max (default): Most sophisticated', which refuses off-topic questions and answers in the user's language; Chatwith warns that 'customizing or overriding the guardrails is difficult'.

Four plans in US dollars, excluding taxes, with annual billing at ten times the monthly price. Starter is $9 a month or $90 a year: 250 message credits a month, about 50 conversations by the page's estimate, 1 agent and 2 million training characters. Hobby is $19 a month or $190 a year with a 7-day free trial: 1,000 credits, about 200 conversations, 1 agent and 10 million characters.

Standard is $99 a month or $990 a year with a trial: 10,000 credits, about 2,000 conversations, 3 agents and 20 million characters. Business is $399 a month or $3,990 a year with a trial: 40,000 credits, about 8,000 conversations, 5 agents and 50 million characters. Sources are unlimited on every plan, and MCP action calls are allowed at 250, 1,250 and 5,000 a month on the plans that include them.

Full pricing notes (2 more paragraphs)

The conversation estimates assume five AI replies per conversation at one credit per reply, and the page says actual use varies by model and conversation length. Add-ons are $9 a month per 1,000 extra credits, $9 per 10 million training characters, $9 per extra agent and $6 per 1,000 MCP actions. Support is community on Starter and Hobby, standard on Standard and premium on Business.

The terms say fees are non-refundable and payment obligations non-cancellable except as the agreement states, you can stop renewal in your account settings and keep access to the end of the period, and the agreement falls under German law with courts in Berlin.

Plans change — always verify the live price on their site.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Fees are non-refundable under the terms, and Starter shows no trial. The gap between Hobby at $19 and Standard at $99 is steep, so small sites may end up buying credit add-ons. Support on the two cheapest plans is community only. The agreement falls under German law. The testimonials and time-saved figures on the site are Chatwith's own. In our own test on 21 October 2026 it answered nine of ten questions correctly from 177 characters of training text and said 'I do not know' to one it could not know, but contradicted itself once ('No. ... so it is still covered') and left our last question at 'Not sent'.

What Chatwith does

No-code AI agent builder that trains a support chatbot on your website, docs, files and connected apps, answers customers on your site, WhatsApp, Slack or Telegram, collects leads and escalates to a person; priced by message credits.

Price the conversations, not the plan

The plans are mostly the same product with more usage. What you are buying is message credits: one credit per AI reply on the default assumption, so Starter's 250 credits are about 50 conversations of five replies, Hobby's 1,000 about 200, Standard's 10,000 about 2,000. Better models and longer chats use more, the page warns. The jump from Hobby at $19 to Standard at $99 is steep, five times the price for ten times the credits plus two more agents.

Before you take it, price the add-on: extra credits are $9 per 1,000 a month, so Hobby plus 2,000 extra credits is $37 for 3,000 credits, our arithmetic, against $99 for 10,000 on Standard. Standard wins once you need more than about 9,000 credits a month or more than one agent. Business at $399 is for volume and several brands: 40,000 credits, 5 agents and premium support. Annual billing gives two months free on every plan, but the terms make fees non-refundable, so pay yearly only once you know your monthly conversation count.

What we found running Chatwith ourselves

Hands-on · 2026-10-21

The first findings are at the top of this page. The rest:

  • All five fact questions were answered correctly, and four of the five rephrased ones, such as 'No. The Lila-17 is purple.' when we asked whether it was red.
  • Asked whether a fault after one month was covered by the 37-day warranty, it answered 'No. The Lila-17 warranty is 37 days, so it is still covered after one month.': it starts with no and ends with yes.
  • Asked about Sunday opening, which our text did not cover, it said 'I do not know the answer to this question.' and made nothing up. Our last question, on return costs, stayed at 'Not sent. Retry', also after a retry; we did not find out why.
  • The test chat made suggestion buttons from the knowledge itself (Product, Color, Pickup, Warranty) and shows 'Powered by Chatwith'.

How to actually use Chatwith

Start on Hobby's 7-day trial rather than Starter, which shows no trial on the pricing page. Train the agent on your help centre, product docs and the files support uses most, then ask it the twenty questions your team answers every week and check each answer against your own policy. If your plan includes them, set up human escalation and lead collection before going live, and watch the analytics for the first week: the number of AI replies per conversation tells you how far your credits will go, which is the real price of this product.

Install the widget through the guides for WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, Wix or Framer, or connect WhatsApp, Slack or Telegram. Fees are non-refundable once paid, so stay on monthly billing until a month of real traffic shows which plan you need. In our test on 21 October 2026 the setup ran in four steps (Train, Brand, Test, Install) and the dashboard stayed closed until they were done. Guardrails start on 'Max', which refuses off-topic questions and which Chatwith says is difficult to override.

Include questions that need a yes or no with a reason: that is where our test agent contradicted itself.

How many conversations does a Chatwith plan cover?

The pricing page, read 13 October 2026, estimates about 50 a month on Starter (250 credits), 200 on Hobby (1,000), 2,000 on Standard (10,000) and 8,000 on Business (40,000), assuming five AI replies per conversation at one credit each. Can I get a refund from Chatwith?

The terms of use, read 13 October 2026, say fees are non-refundable and payment obligations non-cancellable, except where the agreement says otherwise, such as an uncured breach by Chatwith. You can stop renewal in your account settings and keep access until the end of the period.

Is there a free trial?

Hobby, Standard and Business show a 7-day free trial on the pricing page; Starter at $9 a month does not. Read 13 October 2026.
