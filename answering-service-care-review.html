Answering Service Care Review (2026)

Communication & VoiceResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-07

Do the arithmetic before you pick a tier, because their own page does not.

Best for: a small business that loses money when a call goes to voicemail — trades, clinics, law and property firms — and wants real people answering around the clock without hiring a receptionist. It suits you especially if your call volume is genuinely low and predictable, because the plans are priced in minutes and nothing is gated behind a higher tier.
Who should skip it: if your callers mostly want things a human does not need to do — order status, opening hours, a password reset — because you are paying per minute for a person to read them out. Skip it too if your volume is high and variable: at 500-plus minutes you are past the published ladder and into a custom quote, and per-minute billing makes a busy month expensive in a way a flat-rate provider would not.

What the pricing page doesn’t say

The catch

Starter buys the line and nothing else: at $40 plus $1.75 a minute, it stays the cheapest option only up to roughly 79 minutes a month — past that the $179 Small Business plan costs less. The same crossover logic repeats: Small Business gives way to Pro at about 188 minutes, and Pro gives way to Enterprise at about 444. Note too that Small Business is the one tier where an included minute ($1.79 each, $179 for 100) costs more than an overage minute ($1.70), so there is no saving in buying that bundle for its own sake.

And the second thing to price in is the $50 start-up fee, which is easy to miss where they have put it.

What buyers report about Answering Service Care

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026. We have not run Answering Service Care ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Message handling is the part long-term clients single out: reviewers describe getting the caller's name, callback number and the reason for the call delivered by text and email at the same time, which is what they say saves them the time.
  • • Agent quality holds up even in the critical reviews. Several reviewers who complain about billing or answer speed still rate the operators themselves highly, and a number of them name individual agents.
  • • Tenure is unusually visible: reviewers describe 10, 15 and 20-year relationships with the service, and a March 2026 reviewer describes onboarding as responsive, clear and promptly answered.
What they complain about
  • • Calls nobody answered still bill as a minute. A March 2024 reviewer describes robocalls and instant hang-ups arriving on the number ASC provided, each billed as a full minute — roughly 30 minutes of instant hang-ups in the first month — and says the remedy offered was a robocall blocker at about $12 a month rather than a refund. A May 2025 reviewer reports the same one-minute no-agent calls with spam protection already switched on.
  • • Cancelling is not immediate. The same March 2024 reviewer quotes the terms back: a full billing calendar month's notice is required, so the month you decide to leave is a month you still pay for.
  • • The invoices themselves draw complaints. Two March 2023 reviewers call the billing system confusing — one says the bills run backwards and cannot be read — and object to being charged a processing fee for paying by credit card.
  • • Answer and transfer delays come up repeatedly, including from a customer of more than ten years who had to escalate to management about how long the phone rang before it was picked up.

Where the ratings disagree. The score and the complaints are collected differently, so read them separately. Trustpilot shows 117 reviews at 4.1, with 70% five-star and 12% one-star, on a claimed profile with a paid Trustpilot subscription where the company invites customers to review. Most of the five-star entries arrived in an invited wave in March 2023; only four reviews landed in the last twelve months, and the detailed criticism — billing, per-minute charges on hang-ups, cancellation notice — is almost all unprompted. Take the operator praise from the invited reviews and the billing picture from the unprompted ones.

When the paid plan is worth it

Do this sum before you pick the cheapest plan, because the cheapest plan stops being the cheapest at about 80 minutes a month. Starter is $40 and includes no minutes at all -- it is an access fee, and every minute costs $1.75 on top. Small Business is $179 and includes 100 minutes. So 100 minutes on Starter costs $40 plus $175, which is $215: thirty-six dollars more than the plan above it for exactly the same call volume.

The crossover sits at 79 minutes ($179 minus $40, divided by $1.75). Below that Starter wins; above it Starter is the expensive option with a small number on the card. Above Small Business you are buying a lower overage rate as much as a bigger bundle: $1.70 a minute on Small Business, $1.60 on Pro at $329 for 250 minutes, $1.35 on Enterprise at $639 for 500. Paying a year upfront takes roughly 10% off the three larger plans ($161, $296 and $575), and there is a $50 start-up fee that appears only in the prose underneath the table, not on the plan cards.

What the extra money does not buy is capability. Every plan carries the same feature set -- 24/7 cover, bilingual operators, transfers, SMS and email relays, custom greetings, client portal, reporting and the mobile app. You are buying minutes, and there is no long-term contract, so estimate low and move up rather than the reverse.

How to actually use Answering Service Care

Two things shape the first week and neither is on a plan card. There is a one-time $50 setup fee, and the FAQ says what it buys: customising the account, building your initial scripts and configuring routing. That script work is the product -- operators only sound like your business if the call flow is written before go-live -- so treat it as the setup job rather than a fee to grumble about.

You do not need a new number; you keep the one you have and forward it. Then choose how messages reach you, because the options are not equal. Answering Service Care lists the mobile app, the web portal, SMS, email, fax and a phone call, and adds that outbound calls count towards your usage. A habit of asking to be phoned about every message therefore eats the minutes you bought. Usage itself is billed on the actual time spent handling live calls.
