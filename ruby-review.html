Ruby Review (2026)

Communication & VoiceResearched assessment · by Daniel HaketUpdated 2026-09-02

You're paying premium rates for real human receptionists billed per minute — worth it when a missed call is a lost client, but expensive if your call volume is low or an AI answering service would do the job for a fraction.

Best for: client-driven service businesses (law, home services, clinics) where a live human answering every call directly wins business.
Who should skip it: if your call volume is low or mostly routine — an AI answering service or a solid voicemail-to-text setup costs far less.

What the price did

We capture Ruby’s own pricing page every month and keep the figures, so this is our record of the page — not a forecast, and not someone else’s summary.

We captured ruby.com's plans-and-pricing page on 7 July and 2 August 2026 and read it again by hand on 13 August 2026. Every amount is identical in all three: $250, $395, $720 and $1,725 a month for 50, 100, 200 and 500 receptionist minutes; $143, $335 and $520 for standalone chat; $115, $268 and $416 for chat bundled with a receptionist plan. Reviewers elsewhere describe repeated price rises, so this is worth saying precisely: over the five weeks we have measured, the list price has not moved.

What Ruby does

What buyers report about Ruby

Recurring themes from the buyer-review sites listed at the foot of this page, read in August 2026 (App Store reviews read 13 August 2026). We have not run Ruby ourselves, so this is other buyers' experience rather than ours. We summarise it here because the complaints are usually the part a vendor page leaves out.

What they praise
  • • Receptionists sound like your company rather than an answering service — professionalism and friendliness are the consistent praise. Around 4.6 on G2.
  • • Callers get a person, and reviewers report measurably better call handling because of it.
What they complain about
  • • Per-minute billing with no overage warning is the complaint that hurts. One reviewer's bill went from $660 to $5,100 with no explanation offered.
  • • Minutes are not just talk time — hold time and the receptionist's work around the call draw from your allotment. Hangups have been billed at 30 seconds.
  • • Plans run $250 for 50 minutes to $1,725 for 500. At the entry tier that is $5.00 a minute.
  • • Repeat price increases, and reviewers describe service quality slipping while the price rose.
  • • The Ruby 2.0 app carries its own complaint pile in the Apple App Store, separate from the service: seven of the twelve reviews there are one star, and the 2023 migration is the theme — reviewers describe the switch arriving with no notice, a password reset that could not be completed, and the option to send an outgoing text from their toll-free number disappearing in the new version.
  • • A September 2025 App Store reviewer describes the app as slow and effectively unmaintained: messages lagging, call logs slow to load, push notifications failing and no meaningful update in over a year.
  • • One App Store reviewer reports duplicated charges for the same call — the same nerve as the per-minute billing complaints elsewhere, seen from the invoice rather than the plan.

Where the ratings disagree. Around 4.6 on G2 and Capterra against an App Store listing where most reviews are one star. They are not rating the same thing: the software directories rate the receptionists — the humans answering the phone, which is what people praise — while the App Store rates the Ruby 2.0 app introduced in 2023, and those reviews are dominated by the migration and by billing. Both can be true at once. Which one should weigh more depends on whether your team lives in the app or only in the call.

When the paid plan is worth it

Ruby's ladder buys minutes, not features: $250 for 50 receptionist minutes, $395 for 100, $720 for 200 and $1,725 for 500, all read on their plans-and-pricing page again on 19 August 2026. What climbing it actually buys is a lower rate per minute -- $5.00 at the entry plan, then $3.95, $3.60 and $3.45 at 500 minutes. Every plan carries the same live receptionists and the same app, so the tier is a volume decision rather than a capability one.

What upgrading does not solve is the meter. Ruby bills receptionist minutes in 60-second increments and rounds each call up to the nearest 60-second mark, so a run of twenty-second calls costs a full minute apiece on the $1,725 plan exactly as it does on the $250 one. No tier carries an annual-billing discount to soften that. The overage rate is not published either: the FAQs say only that additional minutes bill at the overage rate associated with your plan, so the cost of guessing your volume wrong is a number you cannot look up before you sign.

How to actually use Ruby

Value only arrives once live calls actually reach Ruby, so call forwarding is the opening job. Ruby documents two options. One is forwarding a business number hosted by an outside phone provider, which its onboarding team helps initiate with most providers. The other is letting Ruby host the number, so forwarding is managed from the app and portal. Ahead of that, an Onboarding Specialist walks through normal call handling instructions: what receptionists should do when the owner is available and when they are not.

Daily use then lives at my.call-ruby.com or the iOS and Android app, where the Activity section holds messages that also arrive by email and text. The common snag is meter awareness rather than setup. Ruby bills receptionist minutes in 60-second increments and rounds calls up to the nearest 60-second mark, so brief calls still cost a full minute. The app raises an alert at 85% of plan minutes and again at 100%, and the money-back window closes at 21 days or 500 receptionist minutes, whichever comes sooner.

Distilled from Ruby's own plans-and-pricing page, FAQs page and getting-started onboarding post on ruby.com, read 4 August 2026.

Frequently asked questions

What does a receptionist minute actually cost?

Read on their plans-and-pricing page on 13 August 2026: $250 a month for 50 minutes, $395 for 100, $720 for 200 and $1,725 for 500. That is $5.00 a minute at the entry plan, falling to $3.45 at 500 minutes. Ruby bills in 60-second increments and rounds up, so a 20-second call costs a full minute, and the app raises an alert at 85% and again at 100% of the plan's minutes.

Is there a free trial?

No, and no annual-billing discount either. What first-time buyers get instead is a money-back window that closes at 21 days, or at 500 receptionist minutes or 50 billable chats, whichever comes first. Ruby states there are no activation, onboarding, setup or customization fees.

Is live chat cheaper standalone or bundled?

Bundled, by the 20% Ruby advertises. Standalone chat is $143 a month for 10 chats, $335 for 30 and $520 for 50; added to a receptionist plan the same volumes cost $115, $268 and $416. Both ladders were unchanged between our 7 July and 2 August 2026 captures and our 13 August 2026 read.

What should I weigh before buying Communication & Voice software?

Generally yes, since this category is built for it, but the details that bite are timezone handling, how per-seat cost scales as the team spreads, and whether numbers, routing or coverage exist in the countries your people actually sit in. Availability varies far more by country than the marketing pages suggest, so check your specific regions before committing.
